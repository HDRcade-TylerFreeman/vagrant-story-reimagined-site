HD textures

Graphics › HD texture replacements

These are not upscales. This is new artwork.

Over 2,000 textures, compared here with relief off on both sides. The game's originals were used as a structural guide: the layout, the shapes and how each image maps onto the models and rooms. On that structure the art was generated with AI tools under Tyler Freeman's direction, with surface detail the originals do not have, then chosen and corrected by hand over many passes for seams, skin, cloth, colour and transparency. Material maps and relief have no counterpart in the original game at all.

Two deliberate exceptions: characters keep their original painted faces, cleaned rather than redrawn, and the splash screens are faithful upscales of the originals.

Scenery relief

Graphics › Scenery tessellation

Supported ground, walls and props gain real shape, with separate strength for each. Here it is the first fight, in the rain: each cobble rises and catches the firelight. Collision stays unchanged, and F11 compares it live.

Over-the-shoulder camera

Controls › Camera, or the quick menu (L3)

Classic keeps the game's own view from above. Over the shoulder brings the camera low and close behind Ashley, so you stand in the room instead of looking down on it. Field of view, distance and height are all yours to set, and cutscenes keep the game's own camera.

Real lighting

Graphics › Lighting quality

Real lights, shadows and bounced light on top of the game's baked lighting: from shadow maps up to ray-traced shadows and path-traced light on a capable GPU.

Depth of field

Graphics › Depth of field. Off by default.

The camera keeps Ashley in focus and softens what lies behind and in front, in field play only: off in menus, cutscenes and first person. Five lenses, with a strength setting.

Heat haze

Graphics › Heat haze. Remaster on Vulkan.

The air shimmers faintly above braziers, hearths and big spells. It only reads in motion, so this one is a clip: watch the floor behind the sparks. Recorded at about 12 frames a second, one window grab at a time, so the game runs far smoother than this.

About these images

Every image is a real capture from the game at 3840 × 2160, only cropped or resized. Each comparison is one paused moment with the setting changed live; settings that need a new frame moved the game on by up to a second. The two camera shots show the same fight, not the same instant. A few shots used a test save with debug tools, for example to raise Risk or a weapon's element and to hold enemies still.
